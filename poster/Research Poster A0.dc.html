01

Introduction

This project extends a ready-built FPV drone with an autopilot function and an onboard AI camera that detects landing pads in order to release a payload over them. This is a step towards delivery drones that identify a designated landing spot autonomously, indoors as well as outdoors, where GPS alone is neither accurate nor available enough.

02

Objectives

  • Detect landing pads reliably from a moving drone using onboard vision
  • Integrate autopilot and indoor position hold without GPS
  • Release the payload via a servo-based drop mechanism

05

Mission Sequence

  1. 1
    Command
    The ground station sends the mission command over the ELRS link.
  2. 2
    Search pattern
    The drone flies a preset pattern at hold altitude while the camera streams to the companion computer.
  3. 3
    Detection
    YOLOv11n detects a landing pad. A person in frame blocks the release.
  4. 4
    Approach
    The drone steers towards the detected pad and centers above it.
  5. 5
    Release
    The servo opens the hatch and the payload drops.
On fault
Lost link, no detection within the pattern or low battery: the autopilot returns to launch and lands.

06

Autopilot & Position Hold

Migration from Betaflight to ArduPilot enables indoor position and altitude hold without GPS. Optical flow and LiDAR rangefinding from the MicoAir MTF-01P feed the flight controller over UART; sensor configuration and calibration follow. [Stand ergänzen]

07

Challenges

  • Limited compute on the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W: only a few frames per second
  • Small dataset, restricted lighting and viewing-angle variation
  • Vibration and motion blur during flight
  • Indoor magnetic interference from building rebar caused EKF compass yaw resets, disrupting GPS-denied position hold
08

Conclusion

The drone detects landing pads onboard and releases its payload without relying on GPS. One two-class model proved preferable to two parallel models, since the companion computer cannot carry both. Detection holds across changing light and viewing angles; inference speed is what still limits a fully closed-loop approach.
